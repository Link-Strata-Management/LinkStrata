Thinking of Changing Strata Managers?

Every community is different, but some concerns arise time and time again.

Common reasons Owners Corporations consider a new strata manager

When a Committee begins to feel under-supported, the pressure can build quickly. Strong communication, financial clarity, and proactive maintenance are critical to the smooth running of any community.

Communication has become a challenge

Committee members should not have to repeatedly follow up for responses or updates. Common concerns include delayed email replies, limited updates on ongoing matters, and difficulty obtaining advice when decisions need to be made. When communication breaks down, frustration spreads across the Committee and the wider community.

Financial information isn't clear

Financial management is one of the most important responsibilities of a strata manager. Warning signs can include difficulty understanding financial reports, limited guidance during budget preparation, concerns about levy arrears, unexpected expenses, and little visibility over long-term financial planning.

Maintenance feels reactive

A well-managed community should be planning ahead, not simply reacting to issues as they arise. Common concerns include delayed maintenance, difficulty managing contractors, a lack of preventative maintenance planning, and uncertainty around future capital works.

The Committee is taking on too much

Committee members volunteer their time to support their community. When they are spending increasing amounts of time chasing contractors, managing administrative tasks, following up outstanding matters, interpreting legislation, and coordinating repairs and projects, it may be a sign that additional support is needed.

What should you expect from your strata manager?

  • ✓ Clear and timely communication
  • ✓ Financial transparency
  • ✓ Practical advice and guidance
  • ✓ Proactive maintenance management
  • ✓ Support with governance and compliance
  • ✓ Confidence that matters are being managed appropriately
  • ✓ A partner who helps the Committee make informed decisions
  • ✓ Reduced administrative burden for volunteers

Your strata manager should be helping the Committee make informed decisions while reducing its administrative burden.

Changing strata managers is often easier than you think

One of the most common concerns we hear is: “It sounds like too much work to change.” In reality, the process is often straightforward when properly managed.

Step 1

Initial discussion regarding your community's requirements.

Step 2

Review of current management arrangements.

Step 3

Appointment of LINK Strata Management.

Step 4

Transfer of records, financial information, contracts and documentation.

Step 5

Introduction to your dedicated management team and commencement of services.

Our goal is to ensure a smooth transition with minimal disruption to owners and residents.
